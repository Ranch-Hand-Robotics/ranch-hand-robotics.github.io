# Coming Soon

What could a robot take off your outdoor to-do list? The roadmap starts with practical jobs: scouting a property, moving supplies, and handling heavier chores. It also explores new ways to see what robots see and control them in the field.

The aim is practical ROS 2 robots and tools that are easy to build, customize, and use in the field. These projects are not ready-to-order products; capabilities are design goals and may change as development progresses.

## Sheepdog: a small scout for a big property

A lightweight, modular robot concept for carrying cameras and sensors on routine scouting and homestead monitoring rounds. [Explore Sheepdog's design goals](/sheepdog.html).

## Mule: fewer trips, more done

A medium-duty carrier concept for hauling supplies and handling everyday outdoor tasks. Think smart wheelbarrow, with room for different payloads and attachments. [Explore Mule's design goals](/mule.html).

## Ox: the workhorse for bigger chores

A rugged robot concept for heavier loads and powered attachments across farms, forests, and worksites. [Explore Ox's design goals](/ox.html).

## AI Ground Station: bring the support infrastructure with you

A portable computing and communications hub concept for managing robots and AI applications away from reliable infrastructure. [Explore the AI Ground Station's design goals](/ai_groundstation.html).

## Mixed Reality Bridge: see your robot's world alongside your own

Connect mixed reality devices such as Quest 3 and Android XR to ROS 2 robots. The goal: spatially aligned maps and overlays for working alongside a robot, plus remote teleoperation using Decentralized Robotics transports. [Explore Mixed Reality Bridge's design goals](/mixed_reality_bridge.html).
